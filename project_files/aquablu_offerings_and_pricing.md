## 1. The Business Model in One Line

Aquablu sells **hydration-as-a-service**, not machines. The dominant model is an **all-inclusive subscription** (or **pay-per-drink**) where hardware, installation, filters, CO₂, servicing, and maintenance are bundled into one recurring fee — **no upfront CAPEX, no separate lease with depreciation**. Their own framing: "Forget buying machines." Lease or purchase can be arranged in special cases (large enterprise deals), but subscription is the intended default.

**Why this matters for your CS case:** because revenue is recurring and often consumption-linked (pay-per-drink), *usage = revenue* and *retention = the business*. That's exactly why a Customer Success function — and an AURA-powered health view — is so central to them.

## 2. The Product Portfolio

Aquablu frames three core "products," though two are hardware and one is the software layer:

### 2.1 REFILL+ Series 2 — the flagship (offices & commercial)
- All-in-one **connected water dispenser** for offices/commercial spaces with **25+ employees**.
- Dispenses **hot, chilled, ambient, and sparkling** water plus flavored options — **60+ drink combinations** from a lineup of **8 functional flavors**, all zero-sugar, vitamin- and mineral-infused.
- **Nano-Silver filtration + UV-C purification** removes 99.9% of bacteria, contaminants, and microplastics; multi-barrier (mechanical, chemical, biological) filtration.
- **Two hot temperatures** (≈80°C & 95°C) — replaces the office kettle too.
- **Built-in cellular connectivity** — every dispense streams live to AURA.
- Touchscreen selection with adjustable **flavor intensity**.
- Dimensions ≈ 60×25×18 cm; standalone or built-in; optional design furniture.
- Rated **4.9/5 on Trustpilot**. Dutch-engineered and manufactured.
- Optional **payment terminal** add-on — set prices per drink type (used in semi-public/paid settings).

### 2.2 Aquablu BOLD — the newest (stand-alone, public/high-traffic, consumer-facing)
- Debuted at **CES 2026 (Las Vegas)** — Aquablu's US debut.
- **Fully freestanding, plug-and-play** connected dispenser — no plumbing dependency, so it drops into **gyms, hotels, airports, and public spaces** as easily as offices.
- **6 natural vitamin/mineral flavors** at varying **intensity levels**, plus **functional boosts**: electrolytes, protein, caffeine (collagen coming).
- **Integrated payment options** built in — designed for paid/public deployment.
- Powered by AURA with **automatic inventory management**: when flavors/boosts run low, replacements **auto-ship**.
- Positioned as personalized "hydration as infrastructure."

### 2.3 AURA — the platform (the digital layer, not hardware)
- The **"Hydration Operating System"** connecting all Aquablu units and data.
- For clients/facility managers: **real-time monitoring, usage insights, sustainability reporting** (CO₂ + bottles saved), predictive maintenance, and automated replenishment.
- For end users (with BOLD): app, hydration coach, wearable integration, personalization.

## 4. Pricing — What Aquablu Publishes

Aquablu's pricing is **quote-based and consumption-dependent** — no public fixed price list. But across their pricing and comparison pages they cite these public figures:

### 4.1 Per-drink pricing (the headline metric)
- Marketed **"starting at €0.23 per serving"** (vs Dripl comparison) and **"starting at €0.27 per serving"** (vs Bevi/Quooker comparisons).
- Office subscriptions **"typically range from €0.30 to €0.39 per drink, based on consumption volume"** (for-offices page).
- One engagement page cites **as low as €0.21 per drink** at high volume.
- **Interpretation:** roughly **€0.21–€0.39 per drink**, with the price per drink **falling as volume rises** (classic consumption tiering). Lower per-drink numbers are the "at scale" figures; €0.30–0.39 is the more typical published office range.

### 4.2 Worked monthly examples they publish
- **Team of 100** → ~**4,000 drinks/month** at **€0.27** = **~€1,080/month** (all-in: hardware, installation, CO₂, maintenance).
- **Office averaging 2,500 drinks/month** → **~€850/month** total.
- These are the two concrete monthly figures Aquablu repeats across comparison pages.

### 4.3 What's included in the subscription
Hardware + installation + filters + CO₂ service + maintenance + servicing, and optionally flavoring — **"no hidden costs," no CAPEX, no lease depreciation.** Certified technician install (~1 hour). Roughly **two scheduled maintenance visits per year**; quarterly 12-minute flavor-line cleaning the office runs itself.

### 4.4 The cost narrative (how they justify it)
- Positioned as **up to ~50–60% cheaper** than traditional bottled/canned/coolers; one page claims **up to 78% cost savings**.
- Comparators they cite: bottled water **>€0.60/serving**; retail vitamin water **>€3.00/liter**; traditional cooler delivery **€20–40/month per cooler** before bottles.
- Also leans on a **compliance angle**: under the Dutch Working Conditions Act (Arbowet), employers must provide sufficient workplace drinking water.

### 4.5 Add-ons / variants
- **Payment terminal** on REFILL+ (set price per drink type) — for paid/semi-public settings.
- **Design furniture** / built-in vs standalone options.
- **BOLD** carries integrated payment + functional boosts (electrolytes, protein, caffeine, collagen soon) as premium consumables.

## 5. The Flavor / Consumables Line (the recurring-revenue engine)

Functional flavors are core to both the experience and the razor-and-blades economics:
- Publicly named flavors include **Fresh Lemon, Mango Guava, Red Fruits, Peach, Raspberry & Pomegranate** — plus others (Immunity, Glow) referenced in office marketing; **~8 functional flavors** total on REFILL+ Series 2, **6** on BOLD.

- BOLD adds **functional boosts**: electrolytes, protein, caffeine (collagen soon).
- Consumables (flavors, CO₂, filters) are the **recurring consumption** that the subscription/pay-per-drink model monetizes — and what AURA auto-replenishes.

## 6. Why the Model Matters for Your Business Case

Tie these back to the CS/Aura challenge — they're not just trivia:

1. **Consumption = revenue.** Pay-per-drink means a site's dispense volume *is* the revenue line. A site drinking less is literally earning Aquablu less — so AURA usage data is a direct financial signal, not just an engagement metric. Your account-health wedge is therefore a **revenue-protection** tool, not a nicety.
2. **All-inclusive subscription = retention is everything.** No big upfront sale to recoup; the money is in keeping the subscription alive month after month. That's the entire reason CS exists here and why churn-prevention is the highest-value workflow.
3. **Consumables drive predictable logistics.** Flavors/CO₂/filters must keep flowing; AURA already auto-replenishes newer units. Onboarding + reorder workflows sit at the CS/Ops boundary — a candidate second wedge.
4. **Segment complexity.** Offices, hotels, banquets, gyms, public spaces, and channel partners each have different configs, flavor counts, and pricing (payment terminal or not). A CSM's "account" can be any of these — so any tool you build for CS has to handle heterogeneous account types. Worth acknowledging in the demo.
5. **The pitch is already data-led.** Aquablu sells on "without data you can't measure or report your real impact." CS packaging AURA's numbers into client-facing ESG/value reports is a live workflow — and a plausible thing to surface in your prototype.
